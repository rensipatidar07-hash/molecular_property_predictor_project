## 📺 YouTube Channels & Video Series

- **Dr. Gashaw M. Goshu ("Machine Learning in Chemistry from Scratch")**
    - **Why it's great:** This channel is dedicated *entirely* to what you are doing right now. He has step-by-step video tutorials on using RDKit, extracting molecular fingerprints, and building machine learning models for drug discovery from scratch.
- **Pat Walters**
    - **Why it's great:** Pat Walters is a legend in the cheminformatics industry. He hosts webinars and uploads tutorials talking about practical data science in chemistry. He focuses heavily on what works in the real world vs. what just works in theory.
- **The DeepChem Tutorials**
    - **Why it's great:** DeepChem is a massive Python library built specifically for deep learning in the life sciences. Their YouTube channel and GitHub introductory notebooks are great for when you want to graduate from Random Forests to complex Neural Networks.

## 📚 Books (The Standard Textbooks)

> 📘 **"Deep Learning for the Life Sciences"** > *By Bharath Ramsundar, Peter Eastman, Patrick Walters, and Vijay Pande*
> 
> 
> This is widely considered the "Bible" for beginners entering this field. It was written by the creators of DeepChem. It teaches you SMILES, fingerprints, molecular graphs, and machine learning explicitly tailored to predicting solubility, toxicity, and effectiveness of drugs.
> 
- **"Applied Chemoinformatics: Achievements and Future Opportunities"** *by Thomas Engel and Johann Gasteiger*
    - **Why read it:** If you want to understand the deep chemical theory behind *how* a molecule is digitized, how 3D shapes are stored in databases, and how computer algorithms process molecular structures, this is the academic gold standard.

## 📄 Fundamental Scientific Papers

Reading scientific papers might feel intimidating, but the foundational papers in this specific domain are surprisingly readable and give great context on where your datasets come from.

1. **The Solubility Paper (ESOL):** *"ESOL: Estimating Aqueous Solubility Directly from Molecular Structure"* (John S. Delaney, 2004).
    - This is the exact paper that created the dataset we just used in our python script! It explains exactly how solubility is calculated.
2. **The Benchmark Paper:** *"MoleculeNet: A Benchmark for Molecular Machine Learning"* (Zhenqin Wu et al., 2018).
    - This paper introduces all the popular datasets used globally for Toxicity, Solubility, and Lipophilicity. It explains the standard rules for how to test your AI models.

## 🌐 Blogs & Interactive Notebooks (Where the Community Hangs Out)

- **Practical Cheminformatics (practicalcheminformatics.blogspot.com):** Run by Pat Walters. It's filled with Python code snippets, explanations of new chemical AI tools, and breakdowns of common mistakes beginners make when training models on molecules.
- **RDKit Official Documentation ("Getting Started with the RDKit in Python"):** The official guide on the RDKit website is packed with tiny code examples showing how to draw molecules, calculate weights, mutate structures, and compare chemical similarities.
